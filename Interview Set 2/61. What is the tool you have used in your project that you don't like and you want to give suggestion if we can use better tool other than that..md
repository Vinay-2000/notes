One tool I've worked with that I think has room for improvement is Jenkins. It is very powerful and widely used, but the configuration and maintenance can become complex, especially when there are many plugins and pipelines.

For a new project, I would consider GitHub Actions or GitLab CI depending on the organization's ecosystem. They provide tighter integration with the source-control platform and can reduce some of the infrastructure and plugin-management overhead.

That said, I wouldn't recommend replacing Jenkins just because I prefer another tool. I'd first look at the team's existing infrastructure, migration effort, security requirements, and cost. If Jenkins is already stable and the organization has significant investment in it, continuing with it may be the better decision.

> "In my current project, we've been using Amazon ECS for container orchestration. ECS works well for our current requirements and is relatively simple to operate. However, if the system grows and we need more control over orchestration and deployment strategies, I would consider EKS, which is AWS's managed Kubernetes service.
>
> EKS gives us more flexibility and control through the Kubernetes ecosystem—for example, Kubernetes-native deployments, scheduling, autoscaling, and a much larger ecosystem of tools and integrations.
>
> There can also be cost-saving opportunities with EKS depending on how we design and utilize the infrastructure, especially if we're running a large number of workloads and can efficiently share the underlying Kubernetes infrastructure. But I wouldn't say EKS is inherently cheaper than ECS. I'd evaluate the operational complexity, team expertise, workload characteristics, and actual infrastructure costs before recommending a migration.
>
> So my suggestion wouldn't be to replace ECS immediately. I'd propose evaluating EKS if our requirements have outgrown what ECS provides."

### If they ask: **"Why do you need more control?"**

You can say:

> "ECS abstracts away many orchestration decisions, which is one of its advantages. With EKS, we get Kubernetes primitives such as Deployments, Services, ConfigMaps, custom scheduling, more advanced autoscaling options, and access to the broader Kubernetes ecosystem. That gives us more flexibility when our deployment and orchestration requirements become complex."
